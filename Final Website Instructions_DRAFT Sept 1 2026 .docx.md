**Section 1:** *ABOUT THE NEW PRACTICE*  
**Subtitle:** *From Kusnacht to Puerto Aventuras*

**Header:** *A New Standard in Private Behavioural Healthcare*

**BODY:** *The New Practice is the result of more than twenty five years of experience working with individuals and families affected by addiction, trauma, eating disorders, burnout and other behavioural health challenges.*

*It is also the continuation of a model of care that began in Switzerland more than twenty years ago…*

*In 2007, Lowell Monkhouse began developing The Kusnacht Practice, with the objective of creating an alternative to traditional residential treatment. At that time, most programs were built around group therapy, fixed schedules, and institutional settings. While these models were effective for some people, they were often less suitable for individuals with significant wealth, demanding careers, public profiles, or complex personal circumstances.*

*This new approach is straightforward: rather than asking clients to adapt to a treatment program, the program is built around the client.* 

*That means assembling a multidisciplinary team dedicated to one individual, providing treatment in a private clinical residence rather than an institutional environment, and creating a standard of care that reflects the client’s life experience.*

*It was based on the belief that people recover more effectively when they feel safe, respected, and understood. For someone accustomed to privacy, security, independence, and personal responsibility, removing unnecessary institutional barriers allows them to focus their energy on recovery rather than adapting to an institutional environment.*

*Today, The New Practice builds upon those original principles while reflecting what has been learned over the past two decades. Our model places even greater emphasis on trauma and the inner child as the basis of most of the disorders that we treat, continuity of treatment, family involvement, nervous system regulation, biochemical restoration, and long-term recovery management.*

**Subtitle:** *Trauma-Focused Treatment May Include…*

EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing): *a structured trauma*  
*therapy used to help clients process distressing memories and reduce the intensity of*  
*the thoughts, emotions, images, and bodily responses associated with them.*

Brainspotting: *a focused therapeutic approach that uses visual attention and body*  
*awareness to help clients access and process emotionally charged experiences. It may*  
*be used selectively where clinically appropriate and is integrated within the wider*  
*treatment plan.*

Somatic Experiencing and Nervous System Regulation: *body-based work designed*  
*to help clients notice and regulate physiological responses to stress and trauma. This*  
*can include breathwork, movement, therapeutic bodywork, mindfulness, trauma-*  
*informed yoga, grounding, and other somatic interventions.* 

*Internal Family Systems (IFS) and Parts-Informed Work: helps clients*  
*understand protective, vulnerable, and conflicted parts of themselves with greater*  
*curiosity and compassion, particularly where trauma has created persistent patterns of*  
*shame, avoidance, self-criticism, or emotional reactivity.*

*Attachment-Focused and Relational Psychotherapy: treatment that examines how*  
*early relationships and later interpersonal experiences may influence trust, boundaries,*  
*intimacy, conflict, abandonment fears, and the ability to feel safe with other people.*

*Trauma-Focused Cognitive and Behavioural Approaches: where appropriate,*  
*clinicians may use established cognitive and behavioural methods to address trauma-*  
*related beliefs, avoidance, fear responses, and patterns that continue to interfere with*  
*daily life.*

*Mindfulness, Grounding and Stabilization: practical methods that help clients remain*  
*present, recognize activation earlier, develop emotional regulation, and build the*  
*capacity required for deeper trauma processing.*

*Inner Child and Developmental Trauma Work: for clients whose current difficulties are*  
*connected to childhood experiences, treatment may include carefully structured work*  
*focused on unmet developmental needs, protective adaptations, self-compassion,*  
*boundaries, and reparenting. The objective is not to make a client repeatedly relive traumatic events. It is to help the brain, body, and nervous system process experiences that continue to feel present,*  
*reduce their power over current life, and build a greater capacity for safety and emotional resilience.*

**Section 6:** *Biochemical Restoration*

**BODY:** *Biochemical Restoration is one component of our integrative approach to healing. It is based on the understanding that emotional wellbeing, mental clarity, physical health, nutrition, sleep, stress, hormones, gut health, inflammation, and nervous system function are closely interconnected. When the body is out of balance, psychological recovery is often more difficult. By restoring physical health, we create the conditions that support lasting emotional and psychological healing.*

*We seek to understand the biological factors that may be contributing to anxiety, depression, fatigue, cognitive difficulties, addiction, eating disorders, and other mental health challenges. Our emphasis is on restoring optimal health through nutrition, targeted supplementation, lifestyle changes, sleep optimization, exercise, stress reduction, and, when appropriate, medical interventions.*

*Our goal is simple: to optimize the body’s natural capacity to heal so that clients have the physical resilience, emotional stability, and mental clarity necessary to fully engage in the therapeutic process and sustain long-term recovery.*

**Section 8:** *Inner Child Work*

**BODY:** *For some clients, the difficulties they experience in adult life are rooted in adaptations developed much earlier. Growing up in alcoholic or otherwise addicted homes, homes affected by mental illness, or environments involving emotional neglect, abandonment, instability, chronic conflict, sexual abuse, physical abuse or other forms of trauma can profoundly influence how a child learns to survive, protect themselves and relate to other people.*

*These adaptations may have been necessary in childhood, but later appear as people-pleasing, perfectionism, hypervigilance, emotional suppression, difficulty trusting, fear of abandonment, poor boundaries, compulsive behaviour, codependency or a need to control.*

*Inner Child Work at The New Practice helps clients understand the connection between these early experiences and the reactions that continue to affect their lives today. The purpose is not to remain focused on the past or to assign blame. It is to understand what happened, how the client adapted, and whether those adaptations are still serving them.*

**Subtitle:** *How It Works*

**BODY:** *Working at a pace appropriate to the individual, the client learns to recognise when a present-day situation is activating an earlier emotional wound. A disagreement, criticism, rejection, intimacy, conflict or fear of losing someone, for example, may trigger an emotional response that belongs as much to the past as it does to the present.*

*The client begins to identify the younger, vulnerable parts of themselves and beliefs that may have developed during childhood.*

*The work then moves beyond insight to reparenting. The adult client learns to provide themselves with the reassurance, protection, compassion, boundaries and emotional security that may have been absent during childhood.*

*Rather than asking, “What is wrong with me?”, the client begins asking, “What was this behaviour trying to protect me from, and what do I need now?”*

*At The New Practice, Inner Child Work can be integrated with trauma therapy, EMDR, somatic work, breathwork and other therapeutic approaches.* 

**Subtitle:** *The Benefits*

**BODY:** *Over time, Inner Child Work can help clients develop:*

* *Greater emotional awareness and self-compassion*  
* *Reduced shame and self-criticism*  
* *Healthier boundaries*  
* *Less sensitivity to rejection and abandonment*  
* *Greater ability to tolerate conflict and uncertainty*  
* *⁠Reduced compulsive and self-protective behaviours*  
* *Healthier relationships and attachment*  
* *Greater trust in themselves and their own needs*  
* *An increased ability to respond to life from the resources of the adult self rather than the fears and survival strategies developed in childhood*

**BODY:** *Families often contact us at a time of crisis. *We conduct a comprehensive assessment before recommending a course of action. *Following this assessment we develop an intervention strategy tailored to the individual and their family.*
